# The Titan Ledger

> **Titan is not only maintained. It is built.**
>
> This is the record of the people whose work gave Titan its shape, its
> boundaries, and its sense of direction.

---

## What This Ledger Means

The Titan Ledger is an official record of contribution, not a list generated
from commit counts.

Names appear here with the work that earned their place and the lasting mark
that work left on Titan. A contribution may be code, a test, documentation, an
architectural investigation, an ADR, a bug report, or careful review. What
matters is not the size of the patch. What matters is that the project became
clearer, safer, or more capable because someone gave it their time and
judgment.

> **Recognition follows durable contribution, not ceremony.**

## What Counts as Building Titan

Titan recognizes contribution across the whole system, not only in its runtime
core.

| Contribution | The lasting mark it can leave |
|---|---|
| **Core & API** | A capability that is useful, explicit, and consistent with the contract |
| **Tests & reliability** | Confidence that protects existing behavior and future changes |
| **Architecture** | An investigation or ADR that turns a difficult question into a durable decision |
| **Documentation** | A clearer path for the next developer, without hiding the sharp edges |
| **Review & maintenance** | Careful judgment that keeps the project small, coherent, and honest |
| **Examples & community help** | A practical bridge between Titan's design and the people using it |

An accepted contribution does not need to touch `src/titan/`. In Titan, a
well-reasoned rejection, a precise test, or a documentation correction can
protect the project as meaningfully as a new feature.

## For Everyone Who Comes Next

If you are considering a contribution, you do not need to arrive with a large
feature or a perfect plan. Start with a real problem:

1. Read the [Contributing guide](CONTRIBUTING.md).
2. Read [CONTRACT.md](CONTRACT.md) and the relevant architecture documents.
3. Check the [Roadmap](ROADMAP.md) and existing investigations before proposing
   a new direction.
4. Make the smallest change that solves the real problem.
5. Leave behind the tests, explanation, or decision that helps the next person
   understand it.

Your first contribution does not make you a visitor. Once your work becomes
part of Titan's durable history, it belongs in this ledger.

## How the Ledger Grows

The project maintains this document deliberately. New entries should include:

- the contributor's preferred name or handle;
- the kind of work they contributed;
- the concrete change or decision they shaped;
- the lasting reason future contributors should remember it.

Please do not add a name merely because someone opened a drive-by change, and
please do not measure worth by lines changed. The ledger is meant to preserve
context and gratitude at the same time.

To add or correct an entry, open a focused pull request or raise an issue. The
same review standards that protect Titan's code also protect the accuracy of
its history.
